The Educator's Dilemma

Context & Core Pain Points in Modern Classrooms

🕒

Workload Burnout

Teachers spend over 10+ hours per week designing assessments, grading, and planning lessons. This leaves little time for direct student interaction.

🧩

The Differentiation Gap

Managing classrooms of 30+ students with highly diverse reading speeds, linguistic backgrounds, and accessibility needs is nearly impossible to do manually.

🛡️

The Integrity Crisis

The rise of generative AI has created panic around student cheating. Teachers are trapped in a counterproductive "arms race" using inaccurate plagiarism detectors.

Core Solution Statement: We must transition AI from a threat to academic integrity into an educator's co-pilot that automates grading and planning while preserving human relationship and student agency.

Activity 1: Assessment Prompt Battle

30-Minute Synchronous Session Flow & Prompt Sandbox

How it Works

  1. Assemble: Work in grade-level breakout teams.
  2. Build: Use the interactive prompt builder on the right to select variables, copy, and paste into ChatGPT or MagicSchool AI.
  3. Refine: Critically audit the AI's output. Make at least three modifications to align with student context.

The Differentiated Classroom Challenge

Goal: Differentiate a single core science topic ("How ecosystems respond to natural disturbances") for three distinct student profiles:

Profile A: Struggling Reader (Lexile 600L)

Needs simplified vocabulary, shorter sentences, and visual analogical scaffold.

Profile B: Multilingual Learner (ELL Level 2)

Profile C: Advanced / Gifted Student

08

Academic Integrity & Responsible AI

Dismantling Plagiarism Detectors & Embracing Process

The Fallacy of AI Detection

Traditional AI detectors are highly unreliable, produce frequent false positives, and disproportionately flag writing by English Language Learners. TomoClub strongly recommends abandoning the arms race.

Designing AI-Resistant Assessments

  • 1
    Grade the Process: Grade outlines, research drafts, and reflection logs rather than just final essays.
  • 2
    Oral Defenses: Incorporate short 2-minute slide-walkthroughs or peer presentations.
  • 3
    Hyper-Local Prompts: Connect assessments to classroom discussions or specific local events.

TomoClub Responsible AI Educator Checklist

🛡️ Zero Student PII: Never enter student names or grades into open web AI models.
🧠 Human-in-the-Loop: Never send AI-generated work directly to students without editing.
🔓 Transparency: Clearly define "Green, Yellow, Red" zones for student AI use.

1. Course Specifications & Context

Modern classrooms are faced with a challenging trilemma: overwhelming administrative workload, extreme differences in student learning levels, and deep anxieties surrounding generative AI and academic integrity. This curriculum is designed for TomoClub to bridge this gap, equipping teachers with practical, ethical, and highly effective frameworks to use AI as an active "teacher co-pilot" rather than an administrative replacement or a policing tool.

Session Title Designing Smarter Classrooms: AI Use in Assessments and Personalisation
Target Audience K-12 Classroom Teachers, Instructional Designers, Coaches, and School Leaders. (Highly adaptable for varied technical comfort levels).
Recommended Duration 2 Hours (120 Minutes) in a high-engagement synchronous format, backed by 14 days of asynchronous application.
Expected Takeaways
  • A library of battle-tested, standard-aligned assessment prompts.
  • Two active-built classroom resources (one quiz/rubric, one differentiated lesson scaffold).
  • A school-wide "Responsible AI" safety checklist protecting student privacy.

2. Learning Objectives Framework

The learning objectives are structured intentionally across multiple cognitive dimensions—modeled on Bloom's Revised Taxonomy—to ensure educators do not just absorb theory, but actively build, critically audit, and safely implement AI systems in their schools.

1. Understand (Pedagogical Shift)

Educators will understand the pedagogical shift from rote, recall-based grading to **authentic, process-oriented assessment** that naturally protects academic integrity and amplifies student agency in the age of generative AI.

2. Apply (Co-Creation & Sandbox)

Educators will be able to apply advanced prompting techniques (role-based, context-rich prompting) to **co-create and differentiate standards-aligned assessments, clear rubrics, and personalized reading scaffolds** matching student reading levels (Lexile) or linguistic needs.

3. Evaluate (Critical AI Auditing)

Educators will be able to **critically audit and revise AI-generated outputs** for factual accuracy (detecting hallucinations), demographic or cultural bias, grade-level vocabulary appropriateness, and direct pedagogical alignment.

3. 120-Minute Step-by-Step Session Flow

The session is built using the "Sandwich" active learning model: minimizing passive listening blocks to a maximum of 20 minutes, followed immediately by collaborative hands-on sandboxes. This prevents cognitive overload and caters to different technical comfort levels.

4. Monitoring, Evaluation & Learning (MEL) Framework

To prove the effectiveness of the professional development session, the MEL plan is built to evaluate changes in participant confidence, direct skills transfer, and long-term classroom impacts across both synchronous and asynchronous learning formats.

A. Synchronous Evaluation (During the Live Session)

  • Pre-Session Diagnostics: A baseline pulse poll (Mentimeter) to categorize educator levels (Novice, Explorer, Practitioner, Champion) and track baseline understanding of prompt guidelines.
  • Formative Milestones:
    • Milestone 1 (Prompt Sandbox): Reviewing the quality of prompts created in the Activity 1 breakout. Facilitators evaluate prompt specificity using a simple 3-star checklist (Standard specified? Constraints included? Role defined?).
    • Milestone 2 (Shared Critique Board): Reviewing uploaded materials on Padlet to assess if teachers are successfully identifying and correcting AI factual inaccuracies and bias.
  • Summative Session Check: A digital "Exit Ticket" Google Form requiring teachers to score their confidence on the three learning objectives (1-5 scale) and match standard alignment criteria.

B. Asynchronous Evaluation (PD Certification Performance Task)

To receive full TomoClub professional development credit, participants must submit their Classroom AI Adaptation Folder within 14 days. This ensures that theory transfers to active practice.

Performance Task Deliverables Assessment Criteria & Rubric Indicators Mastery Thresholds
1. Co-Created Formative Assessment
(Quiz, Exit Ticket, or Rubric)		 Clear alignment to a state or national learning standard; inclusion of common student misconceptions as distractor options; clear student-facing language. Standard alignment clearly listed; minimum of 3 distractor rationales provided.
2. Differentiated Scaffolding Set
(Passage or activity with 3 variations)		 Clear target variations (Lexile, language, or enrichment); preservation of rigor (not just lowering standards, but lowering reading accessibility barrier). Three distinct levels with explicit scaffolds outlined.
3. Prompt Log & Reflection Report
(The "Meta-Audit")		 Submission of the exact AI prompts used; 3 bulleted annotations describing specific changes made by the teacher to correct hallucinations, simplify vocabulary, or remove bias. Clear proof of critical editing (AI output was not just blindly copied).

C. Long-Term Impact Indicators (30-90 Days Post-Session)

The ultimate goal is systemic change. TomoClub tracks cohort outcomes long-term using the following metrics:

Workload Reduction

Metric: Self-reported prep time saved.
Target: >75% of cohort reporting saving **3+ hours per week** on routine planning and quiz design within 30 days.

Differentiated Instruction

Metric: Tool usage logs.
Target: >70% of teachers reporting using AI to create at least **two personalized resources** for special education or ELL students monthly.

Assessment Quality Shift

Metric: Assessment design changes.
Target: >60% of teachers reporting moving at least one high-stakes written assignment to a **process-oriented assessment** (oral defense, portfolio, or drafts).

5. Sync vs. Async Adaptation Strategy

The curriculum is designed as a modular framework, allowing seamless transition between live synchronous professional development cohorts and self-paced asynchronous online learning modules.

Synchronous Format (Virtual / In-Person) Asynchronous Format (Self-Paced LMS)
Active Collaboration: Breakout rooms of 3-4 teachers for active role-play. Teachers share screens and construct prompts live. Self-Paced Mini-Videos: 120 minutes is broken into 6 micro-learning videos (5-8 mins each) with embedded check questions (Edpuzzle).
Real-Time Discussion: Interactive pulse polls (Mentimeter) and chat debates on ethics cases. Immediate sharing on a collective Padlet board. Prompt Forums: Teachers post their prompt inputs and generated outputs on a school forum, with mandatory guidelines to peer-review two other colleagues' materials.
Instant Feedback: Facilitator and coaches jump into breakouts to troubleshoot prompt issues, fix errors, and guide teachers. Interactive Sandbox Playgrounds: Built-in prompt builders (like the one in Slide 6) allowing teachers to generate, copy, and test templates.

Verbatim Loom Rationale & Rationale Outline

(Use this outline and the verbatim scripts inside the slide-out Presenter Portal to record your Loom video submission. This direct walkthrough is structured slide-by-slide to answer all required application questions in depth.)

  • Why the session is structured this way (The "Sandwich" Model): The workshop is organized around active learning, minimizing lecture time. Educators are adults with limited attention spans for pure technology lectures; they learn by building. We present 20 minutes of theory, followed immediately by 30 minutes of building, then 25 minutes of differentiation sandbox. This ensures the cognitive load is immediately managed and applied.
  • Why these specific activities were chosen:
    • Activity 1 (Assessment Prompt Battle): Demystifies prompt engineering by giving teachers concrete templates. It immediately proves that AI can generate rubrics and quiz options, saving them hours. The "Battle" element turns grading into a collaborative, creative challenge rather than a solitary chore.
    • Activity 2 (Personalized Pathways): Focuses on empathy and practical equity. By presenting three specific student profiles (struggling reader, ELL student, advanced student), we move personalization from a generic buzzword to a concrete set of Lexile and translation steps.
  • How the assessments connect to objectives: Every single assessment in the MEL framework is anchored to our learning objectives. The Sync Padlet check evaluates their ability to **Evaluate (Audit)** AI bias. The Async Performance Task directly requires them to **Apply** role-based prompts to create a lesson folder, and submit a **Reflection Log** checking their **Critical Evaluation** of the AI.
  • Balancing practical AI use with responsibility: We directly address the elephant in the room—academic integrity. Instead of teaching educators how to use flawed plagiarism software to "catch" kids, we teach them how to design **AI-resistant, process-oriented assessments** (oral defenses, multi-stage drafts). We also provide a strict privacy checklist: never enter student personal identifiable information (PII) into open models.
  • Engaging educators with different comfort levels: We design the material with multiple entry tiers. Novice users are provided with pre-configured dropdown prompt builders (requiring zero typing or coding), while advanced users are encouraged to write custom Claude system prompts. Cooperative group work ensures that technical champions and novices are paired together, fostering community-led learning.
